## TL;DR

- J&L should publish **Option B** — one dedicated FRA service page targeting commercial/local intent ("fire risk assessment Essex", "fire risk assessment London", "fire risk assessment HMO") plus a small cluster of 4–6 supporting blog/resource posts covering landlord obligations, flat-sale conveyancing FRAs, and post-FRA remedials. The remedials angle is J&L's strongest competitive differentiator and is genuinely underserved by current ranking competitors.
- The realistic ranking opportunity in Essex is high. Local SERPs are dominated by national directories (Yell, Bark, Checkatrade) and a small number of regional specialists (Firstford, AJ Fire & Security, Nomico, FireRA, Mast Safety, DC2 Fire). Most of these competitors are content-thin on town-level queries (Brentwood, Romford, Havering, Redbridge) — geographic long-tail is winnable inside 6–9 months.
- Legally, FRAs are mandatory under the Regulatory Reform (Fire Safety) Order 2005 (RRFSO), amended by Section 156 of the Building Safety Act 2022 (effective 1 October 2023), which now requires **all** responsible persons to record the FRA in writing regardless of premises size. The Fire Safety (England) Regulations 2022 (in force 23 January 2023) add specific duties for multi-occupied residential buildings over 11m and over 18m. The Fire Safety (Residential Evacuation Plans) (England) Regulations 2025 (S.I. 2025/797) came into force on 6 April 2026, adding residential PEEP duties on top of this stack. This expanding compliance regime is the demand driver for the service.

## 6. Regulatory and Context Notes (UK, as of May 2026)

### 6.1 Who is legally required to have an FRA

- **Regulatory Reform (Fire Safety) Order 2005 (RRFSO)** — the foundational legislation. Requires the "responsible person" of any non-domestic premises, and the common parts of any multi-occupied residential building, to carry out and maintain a suitable and sufficient fire risk assessment.
- **Housing Act 2004** — separate licensing regime for HMOs. Mandatory HMO licences require evidence of an FRA covering communal areas and means of escape; many councils require BS 5839-1 fire alarm systems to LD2 grade as a licence condition.
- **Single private dwellings** (a house or flat let to a single household) are excluded from the RRFSO. However, best practice and several landlord-insurance policies require one anyway, and any communal area immediately brings the property back within scope.

### 6.2 Building Safety Act 2022 — Section 156 (effective 1 October 2023)

Section 156 amended the RRFSO to:

- Require **all responsible persons** to record the fire risk assessment **in full** (the previous threshold of five or more employees, a licence, or an alterations notice was removed).
- Require the responsible person to record their fire safety arrangements (how fire safety is managed in the premises).
- Require recording of the name and UK address of the responsible person and the identity (and organisation) of anyone appointed to undertake or review the FRA.
- Require multi-occupied residential responsible persons to share relevant fire safety information with residents in a comprehensible format.
- Require cooperation and information-sharing between responsible persons where more than one operates in the same premises.
- Increase fines for relevant offences from Level 3 (£1,000) to Level 5 (unlimited). Per GOV.UK's "Check your fire safety responsibilities under Section 156 of the Building Safety Act 2022" (September 2023): "Section 156 of the Building Safety Act has increased the level of fine for offences… from Level 3 (£1,000) to Level 5 (unlimited)."

### 6.3 Fire Safety (England) Regulations 2022 (in force 23 January 2023)

These regulations operate alongside the RRFSO and impose additional, prescribed operational duties on responsible persons of multi-occupied residential buildings:

- **All multi-occupied residential buildings (regardless of height):** must provide residents with fire safety instructions on how to report a fire and what to do based on the building's evacuation strategy, plus information on the importance of fire doors.
- **Buildings over 11 metres in height:** must undertake quarterly checks on all communal fire doors and annual checks on flat entrance doors.
- **High-rise buildings (18 metres or 7+ storeys with two or more domestic units):** must share electronic copies of the external wall system information and floor plans with the local fire and rescue service; keep hard-copy floor plans and a single-page orientation plan plus the responsible person's UK contact details in a secure information box accessible to firefighters; install wayfinding signage visible in low-light conditions; carry out minimum monthly checks of firefighters' lifts and essential firefighting equipment, with faults not rectified within 24 hours reported to the FRS and records made accessible to residents.

### 6.4 Fire Safety Act 2021

Clarified that the RRFSO applies to the structure, external walls (including cladding, balconies and windows), and individual flat entrance doors of multi-occupied residential buildings — closing the post-Grenfell legal ambiguity.

### 6.5 Fire Safety (Residential Evacuation Plans) (England) Regulations 2025 — new

The Fire Safety (Residential Evacuation Plans) (England) Regulations 2025 (S.I. 2025/797) came into force on **6 April 2026** (confirmed by Essex County Fire and Rescue Service and legislation.gov.uk). These extend duties to require residential PEEPs (Personal Emergency Evacuation Plans) for vulnerable residents in higher-risk residential buildings, sitting alongside (not replacing) the FSER 2022 stack. Any FRA content J&L publishes after May 2026 should reference this regulation explicitly — competitor content that pre-dates it is now incomplete.

### 6.6 EWS1 form (note — not the same as an FRA)

The EWS1 (External Wall System) form is a separate RICS-administered certificate used by mortgage valuers, not a life-safety document. RICS published a second edition of its EWS1 valuation guidance — *"Secured lending valuation of properties in multi-storey, multi-occupancy residential buildings with cladding"* — in May 2026, with an effective date of 1 November 2026 (rics.org, confirmed by Property Industry Eye and Today's Conveyancer). RICS senior specialist for residential valuation Nigel Sellars said: "This updated standard provides practical criteria for when further cladding information is needed, helping to reduce unnecessary EWS1 requests while ensuring that material risks to value are properly considered." J&L should not market FRAs as a substitute for EWS1, and vice versa; both may be required for the same building.

### 6.7 Competence — BAFE SP205 and the National Fire Risk Assessors Register (NFRAR)

While the RRFSO does not prescribe a particular qualification for fire risk assessors, the Fire Sector Federation's "Guide to Choosing a Competent Fire Risk Assessor" (listed on bafe.org.uk) and HM Government's statutory fire safety guidance both reference BAFE SP205 (Life Safety Fire Risk Assessment) as the recognised third-party certification scheme. BAFE SP205 is now at **Version 6.0, published 7 November 2025, effective 31 March 2026** (per bafe.org.uk). The NFRAR Tier 3 standard is the benchmark for HMOs, blocks, higher-risk buildings, care homes and CQC premises. **For J&L's partner contractor model to be credible, that partner should be either BAFE SP205-registered (Version 6.0) or NFRAR Tier 3-listed.** This should be stated explicitly on the FRA service page — it removes the most likely objection and matches what AJ Fire & Security currently lead with.

### 6.8 Pricing context (UK 2026)

Public market data (HSE Property Checks, Clear Fire Compliance, Checkatrade, MyBuilder, FP Fire) places typical 2026 FRA pricing as follows:

- Single-let flat: £150–£260
- Small HMO (up to 5 rooms): £200–£400
- Licensed HMO: £395–£550
- Small commercial premises (under 200 m²): £199–£499
- Standard commercial (cafe, shop, small office): £200–£500
- Larger commercial / multi-storey / care home: £600–£1,500+
- London and South East premium: per Fire Alarm Answers' *"Fire Risk Assessment Cost UK — 2026 Guide"*, "Prices in London and the South East typically run 20–40% higher than the national figures above."

J&L's commercial advantage is not undercutting on FRA price (the partner sets that) but capturing the remedials revenue that follows.
